# Trevor: evaluation harness implementation spec

Companion to `claude/trevor-research.md` and `claude/trevor-corpus-spec.md`. Build this
immediately after corpus phase P0, before P1. Every phase after P0 is then a change with a
number attached rather than an assumption.

---

## 1. Scope, and what this deliberately is not

**V1 measures retrieval only. No LLM is in the loop.** Three measured reasons:

1. **Synthetic questions are valid for retrieval work and invalid for generation work.**
   Kendall τ between synthetic-benchmark and human-benchmark rankings was **0.44 to 0.75 for
   retriever configurations** but ranged **-1.00 to +1.00 for comparing generators**, with
   several metrics inverting model preferences. Your only free labeling source is trustworthy
   for exactly one thing, and this is it.
2. **Retrieval sets the ceiling.** Oracle context scores **76.1 to 88.6%**; closed-book
   **31.5 to 56.1%**. No prompt change moves a 40-point band.
3. **It needs no model, so it never flakes.** recall@k and nDCG@k are arithmetic. It runs in
   CI in seconds and is bit-reproducible.

Generation-quality evaluation is section 8, and it is a later tier, run nightly, not on
every commit.

## 2. The gold set

### 2.1 Anchoring, which is the design decision that matters

**Gold references are line spans, not chunk ids.** A gold item anchors on
`(story_id, line_start, line_end)` from `StoryCommand.line`. At eval time, a chunk is gold if
its line span overlaps the anchor. This makes the gold set survive every rechunking, which is
essential because chunking is a thing you will want to tune and an eval that breaks when you
tune the thing it measures is a ratchet, not an instrument.

```json
{"qid":"g0042","question":"Why does Kal'tsit refuse to explain the Doctor's condition?",
 "anchors":[{"story_id":"main_08-14_end","line_start":412,"line_end":455}],
 "stratum":"causal","source_chunk_id":"main_08-14_end#0007",
 "generated_by":"gemma-4-26b-a4b-q4@sha","reviewed_by":"ian","review":"accepted",
 "closed_book":{"model":"gemma-4-26b-a4b-q4@sha","correct":false,"checked_at":"2026-09-24"}}
```

Freeze as `eval/goldset_v1.jsonl`, reviewed in PRs like source. **Never regenerate it in
CI.** A gold set that is rebuilt automatically will be silently optimized into uselessness.

### 2.2 Construction protocol: known-item generation

Full pooled judging would be 150 queries x ~15 unique chunks x 15 s ≈ **9 hours**. Known-item
generation gets you to the same place in **2 to 3 hours**:

1. Sample chunks stratified across groups, `EntryType`, and story position. Oversample by
   1.5x to survive filtering.
2. Generate one question per chunk with the local model, the source chunk in context.
3. Take the source chunk's line span as the anchor.
4. Spend human time only on rejecting or rewriting bad questions, and on adding missed
   anchors for the ~40 queries where two retriever configurations most disagree.

### 2.3 Anti-leak filters, applied before a human looks at anything

- **Trigram overlap** between question and source chunk **> 0.30 -> drop or paraphrase.** The
  default synthetic-question failure is reusing the chunk's phrasing, which lets lexical
  retrieval win trivially and teaches you nothing about semantic retrieval.
- **BM25 already ranks the gold chunk at position 1 -> drop.** If that holds for more than
  60% of the set, the set cannot distinguish retrievers.
- **Force hardness structurally.** Multi-hop items are generated by sampling two chunks from
  *different stories* that share a gazetteer entity and requiring a question answerable only
  from both. Unanswerable items are written by hand.

### 2.4 The closed-book control, which is the highest-value check in the harness

Run every gold question with **no retrieval at all** and record whether the model answers
correctly. This measures parametric leakage, and for you it is not a formality.

Standard benchmarks leak badly: GPT-5 scored **HotpotQA 50%, 2WikiMultihopQA 62%, QASC 75%**
with no context. When leakage was removed, the spread between competing RAG methods widened
from **3% to 10-18%** — meaning that before removal, the benchmark could barely tell good
retrieval from bad.

**Arknights has a large wiki footprint in pretraining data.** If closed-book accuracy on your
gold set exceeds **30-40%**, the eval is not measuring retrieval and no number from it means
anything. Five lines of harness code.

There is a real tension to name rather than resolve silently: a model that knows Arknights
gives more fluent answers, *and* makes your eval invalid, *and* makes spoiler gating
impossible because it can confabulate post-boundary facts from weights. Measure closed-book
accuracy per candidate model and treat it as a **model selection criterion**, not just an
eval control.

### 2.5 Size and stratification

**150 questions.** That number converges from three independent directions: Sakai's
minimum-detectable-difference of 0.10 gives 75, ARES's prediction-powered-inference floor
gives 150 (below 100-150 it "cannot meaningfully distinguish between RAG systems"), and
Wilson 95% CI half-width at ±7.3 pp gives 150.

| stratum | n | why |
|---|---|---|
| single-fact lookup | 30 | gross-regression tripwire |
| multi-hop across ≥2 stories | 30 | what recall@k is for |
| entity-centric ("what happened to X") | 20 | entity linking, aliases |
| temporal / ordering | 15 | lexical retrieval fails these |
| causal / motivational | 15 | synthesis, not lookup |
| aggregation ("all events where…") | 10 | exposes recall ceilings |
| unanswerable / not in corpus | 15 | abstention; the 13.6-68.4 pp failure band |
| ambiguous entity | 10 | shared names, `???` masking |
| spoiler boundary | 5 | seeded here, expanded in §8 |

**MVP at n=50** is legitimate and ships by Monday, but know what you bought: at p=0.70 the
Wilson 95% half-width is **±12.3 pp at n=50**, ±7.3 at n=150, ±4.0 at n=500. At n=50 you can
detect paired regressions of roughly 9 to 18 pp and nothing finer.

## 3. Metrics

- **recall@10, graded, chunk-level — the single gated primary.**
- **hit@1 / hit@5 / hit@10, binary** — secondary, and the ones whose CI is exact.
- **precision@k of the chunks actually placed in the prompt** — the cheap stand-in for
  distraction-awareness.
- **nDCG@10 — diagnostic for reranker ordering only, never gated.** Retrieval metrics were
  built for a human scanning a ranked list with rank-decaying attention; an LLM ingests all k
  at once. Measured consequence: as the gold passage moves through the ranking, LLM accuracy
  is **U-shaped** while nDCG/MAP/MRR decrease **monotonically**. nDCG is directionally wrong
  about the thing you care about. Separately, eRAG measured Kendall τ between human relevance
  labels and downstream RAG quality at **0.007 to 0.216** — human labels barely predict it
  either.
- **MRR: skip.** It assumes one correct answer at one rank; lore questions are multi-passage.
- **"gold present but answer wrong" counter** — add it once generation exists. It is the
  highest-signal number in the whole retrieval eval, because it is where a reranker or a
  smaller k actually pays off.

Report **per stratum and pooled**. Pooled numbers hide the reranker that fixes factoid lookup
and breaks multi-hop. Per-stratum n is 10 to 30, so per-stratum CIs run ±18 to 30 pp: use
them as directional flags only.

## 4. Statistics and gating

The common failure is `assert recall@10 > 0.72` on 50 queries. With a ±12.3 pp CI that test
fails randomly, and a randomly-failing gate gets disabled within a month.

**Gate on the paired difference against the last green build.**

```
1. Run current and baseline on the SAME frozen query set.
2. Per-query paired differences -> paired t-test + 10,000-sample bootstrap CI on the mean.
3. FAIL  if the 95% CI on the difference lies entirely below 0.
4. WARN  if the point estimate is negative but the CI straddles 0.
5. PASS  otherwise.
```

For binary hit@k use **McNemar's exact test** on the discordant pairs. Keep exactly one loose
absolute gate for catastrophic breakage where noise cannot reach — `hit@10 > 0.40` on 150
queries only fires when the index did not build or the embedding model changed silently.

**Gate on one pooled primary metric only.** Eight strata x four metrics at α=0.05 is 32 tests
and **~1.6 false alarms per run** by chance. Everything else is a non-gating diagnostic. This
is the main cause of eroded trust in eval CI.

## 5. Run manifest

A regression you cannot attribute is a regression you will ignore. Every run appends one JSON
line to `eval/runs.jsonl` keyed by commit SHA:

```json
{"commit":"…","run_at":"…","goldset_version":"v1","goldset_sha":"…",
 "chunker_config_sha":"…","chunk_count":11842,"prefix_enabled":true,
 "embed_model":"gte-modernbert-base","embed_model_sha":"…","embed_dim":768,
 "tokenizer_sha":"…","reranker":"ettin-17m-int8","rerank_top_k":40,"rerank_max_tokens":256,
 "fusion":"rrf","rrf_k":20,"bm25_tokenizer_sha":"…",
 "metrics":{"recall@10":0.731,"hit@1":0.442,"hit@5":0.668,"hit@10":0.731,"ndcg@10":0.598},
 "per_stratum":{…},
 "vs_baseline":{"baseline_commit":"…","recall@10_delta":0.019,
                "ci_95":[-0.004,0.042],"p_paired_t":0.081,"verdict":"warn"}}
```

Track the metric over time, not just pass/fail. Slow drift is invisible to per-PR gates.

## 6. Tiers

| tier | when | runtime | contents |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** golden files | every commit | seconds | chunk count, token deciles, `unresolved.jsonl` = 25, speaker count = 5,808, retrieved chunk ids for a fixed 20-query list, every citation id resolves, cited spans byte-identical to corpus |
| **B** retrieval metrics | every PR | ~1 min | §3 and §4, no model |
| **C** groundedness and answers | merge to main / nightly | ~20 min | §8 |

Tier A catches most real regressions with zero statistics. Build it first; it is a byte-diff.

**Quarantine, do not delete.** A query unstable across three identical runs moves to
`eval/flaky.jsonl` for investigation. Silently dropping unstable queries is how gold sets rot.

## 7. Module layout

```rust
// trevor/src/eval/
mod goldset;   // load, validate, resolve anchors -> chunk ids against a live chunk set
mod metrics;   // recall_at_k, hit_at_k, ndcg_at_k, precision_at_k — pure functions
mod stats;     // wilson_ci, paired_bootstrap_ci, paired_t, mcnemar_exact
mod manifest;  // RunRecord, append to runs.jsonl, load baseline by commit
mod report;    // human table to stdout, JSON to file

pub struct GoldItem { qid: String, question: String, anchors: Vec<Anchor>,
                      stratum: Stratum, closed_book: Option<ClosedBook> }
pub struct Anchor   { story_id: String, line_start: u32, line_end: u32 }

/// A chunk is gold iff its line span overlaps any anchor. This is what makes the
/// gold set survive rechunking.
pub fn resolve(item: &GoldItem, chunks: &ChunkIndex) -> HashSet<ChunkId>;

pub fn recall_at_k(retrieved: &[ChunkId], gold: &HashSet<ChunkId>, k: usize) -> f64;
pub fn paired_bootstrap_ci(deltas: &[f64], iters: usize, seed: u64) -> (f64, f64);
```

`src/bin/eval.rs` takes `--goldset`, `--config`, `--baseline <commit>`, `--out`. Roughly 150
lines of metric code plus the statistics. No async, no model, no network.

## 8. Tier C: the hard parts, later

### 8.1 Groundedness without a paid judge

This costs nothing in quality. On LLM-AggreFact balanced accuracy, **FactCG-DeBERTa-v3-L at
0.4B scores 77.2 against GPT-4o's 75.9**. The 0.4B encoder wins. **HHEM-2.1-Open** is 0.11B,
runs in **under 600 MB and ~1.5 s per 2k tokens on CPU**, and beats GPT-3.5-Turbo by
**+18.12 pp** on RAGTruth-QA. These are encoder forward passes with argmax, so the judge
itself is deterministic and never flakes in CI. Start with HHEM, move to FactCG if you need
the extra point.

Use a local generative model as a judge **only** for binary rubric-anchored verdicts, where
16 small judges across 0.6B to 14B reached **89.55% accuracy**. Never for pairwise preference
or 1-5 scoring: the best binary judge collapsed to **Spearman ρ = 0.21** on MT-Bench, and
Qwen 3 8B showed **position bias of 0.192**. If you do run pairwise, evaluate both orders and
count only consistent verdicts, which converts position bias from a silent error into a
measured disagreement rate. Report **Cohen's κ, not raw agreement**: exact match
**overstates chance-corrected agreement by 33.8 to 41.3 pp**.

One trap to avoid by construction: **never use the same model to generate relevance labels
and to rerank.** When judge and reranker are the same model, system-level Kendall τ collapses
to 0.63 overall, **0.44 on the top 20 systems and -0.40 on the top 5** — the eval starts
rewarding the reranker for agreeing with itself, and it does so exactly where you are trying
to tell good from slightly better.

### 8.2 Citation accuracy

Use the ALCE definitions verbatim. Citation recall is 1 for a statement iff it carries ≥1
citation and the **concatenation** of cited passages entails it; citation precision penalizes
a citation that neither supports the statement alone nor changes the remaining citations'
ability to support it. Measured human agreement of the automatic procedure: recall
**κ = 0.698, 85.1% accuracy**; precision **κ = 0.525, 77.6%**. So recall is trustworthy and
**precision deltas below ~5 pp are noise**.

Substitute FactCG-DBT (0.4B) for ALCE's T5-11B entailment model. Since it scores above GPT-4o
on the task, the substitution is an upgrade rather than a compromise.

Free per-commit pre-filter with no model at all, in Tier A: every citation id must exist in
the retrieved set, and every cited span must be byte-identical to corpus text. Dangling
citation ids catch a surprising share of real bugs.

### 8.3 Spoiler filter

**Start with the negative result, because it saves weeks.** A 2026 evaluation of ContextCite,
AttriBot, TracLLM and TokenShapley found they **cannot disentangle in-context from in-weight
contributions** — a genuinely supporting span can score as unimportant. Do not try to prove
spoiler-safety by attribution.

The design that works is counterfactual, and it reuses §2.4's machinery:

1. **Closed-book control.** If the model already knows the post-boundary fact from weights,
   the filter cannot work and the problem is model choice, not retrieval.
2. **Filtered condition.** Normal pipeline, filter on. Did the answer leak?
3. **Oracle-leak condition.** Filter off, to confirm the question is answerable at all and to
   calibrate the detector.

Leak detection needs no generative judge: you already know the spoiler fact because you built
the question around it. Encode it as spoiler claims and run the entailment model with
`premise = answer`, `hypothesis = spoiler claim`. Add a gazetteer backstop for spoiler
entities to catch paraphrase misses.

**Report two numbers or you are measuring one side of a tradeoff.** Leak rate, and
over-filtering rate on questions that *are* answerable pre-boundary. A filter with 0% leak
and 40% over-refusal is a broken product.

And state leak rate as a **Wilson upper bound, never as "0%"**. With 50 probes and zero
observed leaks the honest claim is **≤7.1%**; with 150 probes and zero leaks, **≤2.5%**. That
is the correct way to state a safety property from a finite sample.

### 8.4 Summarization faithfulness

All the metrics above are reference-free, which is what you need since no gold summaries
exist. But long narrative documents break them specifically: a 2026 stress test found
AlignScore penalizes *correct* abstractive rewording, UniEval fails on logically equivalent
negation, and the root cause is **not length but high claim density with evidence dispersed
across the document** — precisely the "this character's arc across six stories" case.

Recipe:

1. Decompose the summary into atomic claims with a local model. This is the one place a small
   generative model earns its keep, because it is a generation task, not a judgment task. Key
   the cache by summary hash so it is deterministic across runs.
2. Per claim, retrieve top-m evidence sentences from the source and **expand by ±1-2
   neighboring sentences** — that expansion is the paper's own measured mitigation — then run
   entailment.
3. Report the **distribution**, not the mean: percent fully supported, partially, unsupported.
   The mean hides the one fabricated claim that matters.
4. Report the **coverage counter** from the corpus spec alongside it. Faithfulness is
   one-sided and a summary that says nothing scores 100%.
5. Anchor with 30-50 human-judged summaries and report κ between the metric and your own
   judgments. The human-annotator ceiling in this literature is **Pearson 0.70**; know where
   your ceiling is before chasing a two-point delta.

## 9. The minimum viable version, one weekend

1. 50 questions, known-item generated, ~15 minutes of manual rejection.
2. Trigram filter at 0.30, drop BM25-rank-1 questions.
3. Closed-book control. **If it exceeds 30%, stop and fix the eval set before trusting any
   retrieval number.**
4. hit@1, hit@5, hit@10, recall@10, nDCG@10.
5. Paired t-test plus 10,000-sample bootstrap CI against the previous run; append to
   `runs.jsonl` keyed by commit SHA.
6. Gate only on the 95% CI of the paired difference lying entirely below zero.

At n=50 this detects paired regressions of roughly **9 to 18 pp**. Coarse, but it is a real
number with a real error bar, and it exists before P1 spends 3.5 hours of Mac time on
contextual prefixes that you would otherwise have no way to justify keeping.
