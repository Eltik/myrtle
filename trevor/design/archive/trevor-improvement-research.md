# Trevor: what would make it answer real lore questions better (research, 2026-09-28)

The question: which methods have the best evidence for raising Trevor's answer quality on real player questions, ranked by expected gain per hour, under Trevor's constraints (no external LLM APIs; one MacBook Pro M5 Pro with 24 GB; Gemma 4 12B QAT generating at about 18 to 22 s per answer, Qwen3.5 9B judging, gte-modernbert-base INT8 at about 0.12 s per chunk, Ettin reranker, tantivy BM25; the VPS serves precomputed answers and retrieval only). "Train" is read loosely, so fine-tuning is covered, but most of the ranked list is retrieval, routing and prompting, because that is where the evidence and Trevor's own measurements point.

## 0. The starting point, and how to read the evidence

Trevor's own numbers (design/trevor-questions.md section 8, design/trevor-retrieval-baseline.md sections 8 to 20): on 120 real r/arknights questions `ask` responds 0.550 (P3a) to 0.575 (P3b, +0.025, CI [-0.008, +0.067]); faithfulness 0.806 to 0.826 against 0.971 on gold set v2; reading order 0.29 (n 7), canon 0.20 (n 5), source text 0.14 (n 7), plot 0.33 to 0.50 (n 6); relationships and fate 0.83; worldbuilding and races 0.70 to 0.73. On the 68 story-answerable real questions, 45 to 48 get a response, and most misses are declines where the answer exists. On gold set v2, 84/131 correct, with about 24 misses where the gold passage never reached the model and multi-hop the weakest stratum (14/30 at first scoring).

Two cautions shape the ranking. First, per-category probe numbers are very noisy: 2 of 7 has a Wilson 95% interval of about [0.08, 0.64], so a category moving from 0.29 to 0.57 on n 7 is two questions. Every experiment below is judged on paired runs over the whole 120 (and on the Discord set as a held-out check), not on one category's rate. Second, most published gains were measured with far larger models (GPT-4, PaLM-2L, Llama 70B) on English Wikipedia or web QA, where the generator has seen the facts in pretraining. Trevor's generator answers only 0.130 of gold v2 closed-book, so methods that lean on the model's prior knowledge (HyDE, Query2doc, step-back abstractions of the answer) transfer worst here. Trevor has already refuted two literature favourites on its own data: contextual prefixes on embedded text (dense recall@10 -0.087, section 8) and the Ettin-17m reranker at depth 40 (hybrid 0.714 to 0.651 on P1a). Measurements on Trevor win over papers.

## 1. Ranked experiments

Costs assume one model loaded at a time; "a probe run" is the 120 real questions through `ask` (about 40 to 45 minutes of Gemma) plus `scripts/real-probe.py judge` on Qwen; "a gold run" is gold set v2 through `ask --batch` (146 questions, about 50 minutes) plus `scripts/answer-eval.py`. Retrieval-only measurements (`eval run`, recall@10) take seconds.

### 1.1 Reading-guide route (targets N, reading order, 0.29; 8.6% of Reddit and 5.2% of Discord questions)

**Build.** A table route in `ask`, like the listing and attribute routes: "where do I start", "what should I read before X", "when do I read Babel", "is X before Y" answered from data Trevor already holds (chronology v1 in-world order, event release order from `spoiler.jsonl`, each event's primer prior groups, main episodes in order), with no generation. The one data gap is EN dates for Episodes 0 to 9 (section 6 of the questions doc); the route can state the main-story order and place events by their primer prerequisites without those dates, and say so.

**Expected effect.** This is engineering, not a literature result, but Trevor's own route history is the evidence: the attribute route answered "operators from Kazimierz" with 17 where retrieval found 4 (baseline section 20), and the listing route replaced a six-item retrieved "order" with the full 449-group chronology (section 16). Retrieval cannot answer "what order" questions because no passage states the order. A plausible outcome is N responds from 2/7 to 5 or 6 of 7, which is about +0.03 on the whole probe, and it makes the answer correct by construction rather than merely responsive.

**Cost.** 4 to 6 hours of code, no generation. **Measure.** Add 10 reading-order questions to `eval/routes.jsonl` and pass `scripts/route-check.py`; rerun the 7 N questions of the probe and sweep `ask --route-only` over all 1,102 real questions to count misfires, as was done for the other routes (9 table answers before the event-list guard, 3 wrong). **Kill switch.** The existing `--no-route`, plus a route-specific env var whose off value reproduces the current route-check output byte for byte.

### 1.2 Policy lines for canon, cross-media, visual and opinion questions (targets K 0.20, S 0.40, Q 0.43, M 0.62; together 30% of Reddit questions)

**Build.** Prompt lines, selected by a keyword route like the existing synthesis instruction: for canon ("is IS canon", "which ending is canon") say the game does not rule on canon, then report what the retrieved text shows and whether a later story references it; for cross-media (Endfield, anime) say what the game text covers and stop; for visual questions say that Trevor reads text, not art, and give any text description; for opinion and power give the evidence on each side. The probe already shows the failure shape: handles 42/43, so Trevor rarely invents a verdict, but it declines flatly, which the responds judge counts as a miss.

**Expected effect.** The evidence is internal: the declines are the misses (questions doc section 8), and the synthesis instruction for comparison questions turned Ian's "rank the most powerful Sui" from a decline into an answer (baseline section 15). Expect K, S and Q responds to rise and handles to stay at 42 or 43 of 43; the risk is that a "here is what the text shows" answer cites unrelated passages, which is the IS3-endings failure (the folk-tale citation), so faithfulness on these categories (K 0.33 today) is the number to watch.

**Cost.** 1 to 2 hours plus one probe run. **Measure.** Paired probe run, responds and handles on the 43 policy questions, faithfulness on K. **Kill switch.** A `--no-policy` flag that restores the current system prompt (prompt sha unchanged).

### 1.3 Measure, then fill, answer-bank coverage against the real question mix (the serving lever for every category)

**Build.** The site cannot generate, so what a user sees is mostly (bank hit rate x bank answer quality) plus retrieval-only fallback. Nobody has measured the hit rate on real phrasing. Step one embeds the 1,102 real questions (about 2 minutes) and reports, per category, how many match a bank entry at cosine 0.95 and at 0.90, and whether the matched entry answers the question (Qwen, one verdict each, about 20 minutes). Step two fills the gaps: cluster the real questions (Reddit as the build set, Discord's 345 held out), and for each cluster generate 5 to 10 paraphrase questions in the real register ("Can Sankta fly?", not the generated-bank style "What does X say to Y in story Z?"), answer them with `ask`, and keep answers that cite and pass the sentence-support filter (1.9). Priority is cluster size times story-answerability times current miss.

**Evidence.** PAQ generated 65 million QA pairs and showed that a QA-pair retriever that backs off to a full retrieve-and-read model when unsure is "more accurate and 2x faster than the state-of-the-art model alone", while a closed-book model trained on the same pairs underperforms explicit retrieval by over 15% ([Lewis et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.07033)): a bank with a fallback is sound, a bank alone is not. Semantic caches in customer service reach 61.6% to 68.8% hit rates with over 97% of hits correct ([Regmi and Pun 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.05276)), but those query streams are far more repetitive than lore questions, so Trevor's hit rate will be lower and has to be measured. Doc2Query-- showed that filtering hallucinated generated questions improved retrieval by up to 16% and cut the index by 33% ([Gospodinov et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.03266)): generated questions need a filter, which the bank already has (cite, no decline) and should extend with the support filter.

**Cost.** Measurement about 1 hour. Filling: 1,000 new entries cost 1,000 x (1.7 s question + about 19 s answer), about 6 hours of Gemma, plus judge time. **Measure.** Bank hit rate and matched-answer acceptance on the held-out Discord questions, before and after. **Kill switch.** New entries carry a `batch` tag; serving filters it out, and the bank file without the tag is the current 3,107 entries.

### 1.4 Summary-first context for plot, ending and topic questions (targets B plot 0.33 to 0.50, H worldbuilding 0.70, R races 0.64 to 0.73)

**Build.** Two parts, both RAPTOR-shaped but reusing the hierarchy Trevor already has (story summaries, method-C event summaries, dossiers). (a) When a question names an event, episode or story (the route already resolves names for the death routes), put that group's event summary and the relevant story summaries (the last story's for "ending" questions) first in the context, then retrieved passages; this is RAPTOR's "collapsed tree" (summaries and leaves retrieved together), which its authors found beat tree traversal. (b) Topic dossiers for the most-asked races, nations and concepts (Sankta, Sarkaz, Aegir, Sami, Originium, Catastrophes, mobile cities), picked from the clusters in 1.3, built like character dossiers from the passages and summaries that mention the topic, and added as P3b-style profile units.

**Evidence.** RAPTOR's large number (+20.3 points on QuALITY to 82.6%) comes from GPT-4 as the reader; with a 3B reader the gain was +1.7 accuracy on QuALITY (54.9 to 56.6) and +1.6 ROUGE-L on NarrativeQA ([Sarthi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.18059)). A standardized comparison with Llama-3.1-8B found RAPTOR below plain RAG on single-hop NQ (60.04 against 64.78 F1) ([Han et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371)). Trevor's own P3b run is consistent with a small, real effect: plot 0.33 to 0.50, races 0.64 to 0.73, but gold v2 retrieval -0.015 because profile units displaced story passages for 2 questions. That is why part (a) routes summaries in by name rather than letting them compete in the flat index, and why summaries go first (1.5 explains the position effect).

**Cost.** Part (a): 3 to 4 hours of code, no generation. Part (b): 50 topics at about 16 s each (the dossier rate) is under 15 minutes of Gemma, plus a faithfulness sample on Qwen (about 1 hour). **Measure.** Paired probe (B, H, R), gold v2 answers to check the displacement does not recur, gold v2 retrieval on story passages. **Kill switch.** `--no-summary-first`; topic units live in a separate corpus dir, so `--corpus artifacts/p3b` is the old behavior.

### 1.5 Passage budget sweep with reordering (targets synthesis misses across B, H, R, and the 24 gold v2 retrieval misses)

**Build.** `ask` uses k 8 with one neighbour on each side, capped at 9,000 passage tokens. First compute, with no generation, hybrid recall at 8, 12, 16 and 24 on gold v2 (the ceiling a bigger budget can reach). Then answer arms at k 12 and 16 (caps about 13,000 and 17,000 tokens), with passages reordered so the best-ranked sit at the start and end of the context rather than the middle.

**Evidence.** This is the one method whose best evidence was measured on Trevor's model size. Jin et al. found an inverted U with Gemma-2-9B and Mistral-Nemo-12B: output improves with more passages and then declines, driven by hard negatives, and reordering passages to the ends "significantly and consistently outperforms the original ordering when the number of retrieved passages is large" ([Jin et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05983)). In "Lost in the Middle", GPT-3.5-Turbo with 20 documents scored 75.8% with the answer first and fell to about its 56.1% closed-book level with the answer mid-context ([Liu et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.03172)). Databricks' 2,000 runs over 13 models found most degrade past 32k to 64k tokens ([Leng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.03538)), so Trevor's 9k to 17k range is well inside where extra passages can help. Trevor's own number: the gold passage reached the model for 105 of 131 questions, so up to 26 are recoverable in principle.

**Cost.** Recall curve: minutes. Each answer arm: a gold run and a probe run, about 1.5 to 2 hours of Gemma plus judging; longer prompts add a few seconds per answer. **Measure.** Gold v2 correct (paired), gold-in-passages count, faithfulness; probe responds. Stop at the first k where correct drops. **Kill switch.** `--k 8 --max-passage-tokens 9000` and a `--no-reorder` flag reproduce today's prompts byte for byte (checkable by prompt hash).

### 1.6 Retry-on-decline with a rewritten query (targets the "declines where the answer exists" misses: 20 of the 68 story-answerable real questions; false premise I 0.50)

**Build.** Only when the first answer declines (adaptive, so most questions pay nothing), ask Gemma for two or three rewrites in the story's own terms: a step-back version ("Can Aegir breathe under water?" to "Aegir physiology and life in the sea"), a premise-free version ("When did Ceobe get high on drugs?" to "Ceobe eats mushrooms and hallucinates"), and key-entity variants. Retrieve each, fuse with the existing RRF, answer once more. Rewrites go to retrieval only, never into the answer context.

**Evidence, and why not HyDE.** Step-back prompting gave +7 and +11 points on MMLU physics and chemistry, +27% on TimeQA and +7% on MuSiQue, with PaLM-2L, GPT-4 and Llama2-70B ([Zheng et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06117)). Query2doc raised BM25 by 3% to 15% on MS MARCO and TREC DL ([Wang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.07678)), and HyDE lifted unsupervised Contriever from 44.5 to 61.3 nDCG@10 on DL19 ([Gao et al. 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.10496)). But HyDE's gain is against an unsupervised encoder; against the fine-tuned ContrieverFT it ties on DL19 (61.3 against 62.1) and loses on DL20 (57.9 against 63.2), and gte-modernbert is a supervised retriever. Both HyDE and Query2doc write a pseudo-answer from the model's own knowledge, which for Gemma on Arknights is right 13% of the time, so the pseudo-documents will carry invented lore. RAG-Fusion reported off-topic answers when generated queries drift from the original ([Rackauckas 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.03367)), and Trevor's alias-expansion test lost the parents chunk for the Haruka question (baseline section 11). Adaptive-RAG's point, route only the questions that need the expensive path, is the design here ([Jeong et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14403)). Expected effect is modest: perhaps a third of retried declines turn into answers, which is about +0.03 to +0.05 responds on the probe, and it must not un-decline the 15 unanswerable gold items.

**Cost.** 3 hours of code; retried questions cost about 2 s for rewrites plus a second 20 s answer. One probe run and one gold run. **Measure.** Probe responds (paired), faithfulness, gold v2 correct and unanswerable declined (must stay 15/15). **Kill switch.** `--no-retry`, default on only after it passes; off reproduces current output exactly because the retry path never runs.

### 1.7 Fine-tune the embedder on in-domain pairs (targets retrieval misses everywhere; dense is the weak leg, 0.566 against BM25 0.681 on gold v2)

**Build.** Generate one or two questions per chunk for script and archive chunks outside the gold v2 stories (the bank's 900 questions are a start), mine hard negatives from BM25 top 20, keep pairs where Qwen or the reranker confirms the positive (GPL-style pseudo-labels), and fine-tune gte-modernbert-base with MultipleNegativesRankingLoss in sentence-transformers on MPS. Re-export INT8 ONNX, re-check the Apple Silicon cosine against fp32 (0.997 today) and the fp32 export for the VPS, re-embed 15,762 chunks.

**Evidence.** GPL gave up to +9.3 nDCG@10 on six domain datasets from generated queries and cross-encoder pseudo-labels ([Wang et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07577)). A worked example fine-tuning bge-base (same 768-d base-size class) on about 6,300 synthetic pairs from one 10-K filing raised nDCG@10 from 0.768 to 0.825 in under 4 minutes of GPU ([Schmid](https://www.philschmid.de/fine-tune-embedding-model-for-rag)). Arknights names (Kal'tsit, Ch'en, Ifrit-Nian) and in-world terms are exactly the out-of-domain vocabulary where dense retrievers trail BM25. The trap specific to Trevor: gold v2 was written by the same Gemma question generator, so training on Gemma-generated questions will look better on gold v2 than it is. The real probe and the archive slice are the honest checks, and gold v2 stories must be excluded from training.

**Cost.** 10,000 questions at 1.7 s: about 4.7 hours of Gemma; filtering about 1 to 2 hours of Qwen; training a 149M model: under an hour; re-embed about 30 minutes; ONNX export and parity check 1 to 2 hours. About 9 to 10 hours in all. **Measure.** Gold v2 dense and hybrid recall@10 (seconds, paired bootstrap, gate CI excludes zero), archive slice, then one probe run if hybrid passes. **Kill switch.** `--model-dir models/gte-modernbert-base` with the old vectors; the new model lives in its own dir and corpus vectors are sha-pinned.

### 1.8 Two-round iterative retrieval for multi-hop, offline only (targets gold multi-hop 14/30 and real "why/how did X lead to Y" questions)

**Build.** For questions the router marks as multi-part (two named entities, "why", "after", "because"), let Gemma write what it still needs after the first passages ("which story shows Manfred after the war?"), retrieve once more, and answer from both sets under the same passage cap. Offline only (bank building and the gold eval), since latency doubles.

**Evidence.** IRCoT improved retrieval by up to 21 points and QA by up to 15 on HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, MuSiQue and IIRC, including with Flan-T5 readers ([Trivedi et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10509)), so it does not need a frontier model. HippoRAG matched IRCoT in one retrieval step at 10 to 30 times lower cost using an entity graph with Personalized PageRank ([Gutiérrez et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14831)), but it needs OpenIE triples over the whole corpus. All of these benchmarks are Wikipedia multi-hop, where the hops are explicit entity bridges; Trevor's multi-hop items bridge across stories and chapters, which is less studied.

**Cost.** 4 hours of code; about 40 s per routed question; one gold run (only the multi-hop-routed subset pays). **Measure.** Gold v2 multi-hop stratum and overall correct (paired), gold-in-passages count. **Kill switch.** `--rounds 1` is today's path.

### 1.9 Sentence-level support filter on bank answers, and a second judge (targets faithfulness 0.81 on real questions)

**Build.** Every generated bank answer goes through the support judge sentence by sentence, as primers already do (1,090 of 1,123 primer sentences kept, section 17); unsupported sentences are dropped, or the answer is regenerated once with them named. Separately, the synthesis prompt should mark inference explicitly ("the story implies"), because the real-question gap (0.806 against 0.971) comes from synthesis sentences that no single passage states.

**Evidence and the instrument problem.** Small dedicated checkers can match GPT-4 at grounding checks: MiniCheck (770M) reached GPT-4-level accuracy on LLM-AggreFact at 400 times lower cost ([Tang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10774)), so a 9B judge is not obviously too weak for one-sentence support verdicts. But filtering with Qwen and then measuring faithfulness with Qwen is circular: the number will approach 1.0 by construction. The measurement has to come from a different judge (Gemma judging Qwen-filtered answers is a different family; a panel of smaller judges beat a single GPT-4 judge in [Verga et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18796)) or from Ian's hand labels. Note also that faithfulness here is a lower bound, since the judge sees only the passages given, and cross-passage synthesis is under-credited (baseline section 9.4).

**Cost.** About 3 to 5 s per answer sentence on Qwen; for 3,000 bank entries of about 3 sentences, 8 to 12 hours, so run it on new entries and a sample of old ones. **Measure.** Faithfulness on the probe by a second judge; the share of answers that lose a sentence; responds must not fall. **Kill switch.** An env var like the existing `PRIMER_FILTER=0` keeps whole answers.

### 1.10 Index the missing typed sources with source honesty (targets J source text 0.14; 5.3% of Reddit questions)

**Build.** Module stories (259k words), IS texts (181k), enemy handbook (68k) and voice lines (314k, one unit per operator) as typed units with a source field, the way `build-archives` added operator files, plus a prompt rule: when the question names a source type, answer only from units of that type or say it is not indexed. The probe's worst failure (a module question answered from story passages as if they were the module) is fixed by the rule even before indexing.

**Evidence.** Internal: P3a added 1,925 archive chunks with hybrid on story questions unchanged (0.747 to 0.747) and archive questions at 0.867 recall@10 (baseline section 10), the template for adding sources without regressing. At about 500 tokens per chunk, the 822k words are roughly 2,000 to 2,500 chunks, about 5 minutes of embedding. The cost is spoiler gating (IS by run content, modules by promotion, voice lines by trust), not compute.

**Cost.** 6 to 10 hours of code including gates; minutes of embedding. **Measure.** A small automated archive-style gold slice per source type (the P3a method), gold v2 hybrid must not drop, probe J. **Kill switch.** A separate corpus dir, as P3a.

### 1.11 A larger reranker as a passage selector, at shallow depth (targets the 26 gold questions whose passage did not reach the model)

**Build.** Rerank the hybrid top 20 to 30 with a larger cross-encoder (a 150M-class Ettin checkpoint, or a 568M model such as bge-reranker-v2-m3), and use its order only to choose which 8 passages enter the prompt.

**Evidence.** Rerankers improve as they score more documents and then degrade past a point ([Jacob et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11767)); Trevor's Ettin-17m at depth 40 already lost 0.022 to 0.063 recall@10 (baseline sections 3 and 8), which is consistent with a small model at too great a depth. Anthropic's 67% failure-rate reduction includes reranking, but on top of their own embeddings and with a commercial reranker ([Anthropic 2024](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)), so it does not transfer as a number. Lower in the ranking because the 17m result is negative and a larger model costs seconds per query on the VPS CPU.

**Cost.** 3 hours; latency measured per model. **Measure.** Gold v2 recall@10 and hit@1 at depths 20 and 30 (seconds each), gate CI excluding zero. **Kill switch.** `--rerank-top 0`, today's default.

### 1.12 Behavior LoRA on the generator (last; see section 2)

Only if 1.2, 1.6 and 1.9 leave a behavior gap that prompts cannot close (calibrated declines, the policy lines, citation habits). Section 2 prices it.

## 2. Fine-tuning the generator: feasible, expensive, and aimed at behavior only

**Feasibility on 24 GB.** mlx-lm trains LoRA, DoRA or full fine-tunes, and switches to QLoRA automatically when the base model is quantized; the memory levers are batch size (default 4), number of adapted layers (default 16), gradient accumulation and gradient checkpointing ([mlx-lm LORA.md](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/LORA.md)). The documented speed is about 250 tokens per second on an M1 Max with 32 GB and 475 on an M2 Ultra for a 7B model ([mlx-examples](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-examples/blob/main/lora/README.md)). Unsloth lists QLoRA memory for Gemma 4 as 10 GB for E4B and 22 GB for 31B ([Unsloth](https://unsloth.ai/docs/models/gemma-4/train)), so a 12B QLoRA should land around 10 to 14 GB with batch 1, which fits 24 GB only with nothing else loaded and short sequences. Trevor's RAG prompts run up to 9,000 passage tokens, and activation memory grows with sequence length, so training examples would need 3 to 5 passages (about 3,000 to 4,000 tokens), not the full serving context. Gemma 4 12B QLoRA with mlx-lm is reported by users ([Wong](https://medium.com/@able_wong/part-3-fine-tuning-gemma-4-on-a-mac-mini-and-what-the-results-taught-me-4200ce5523e2)); treat that as an anecdote.

**Deployment wrinkle.** mlx-lm's GGUF export covers only Mistral, Mixtral and Llama-style models in fp16 ([mlx-lm LORA.md](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/LORA.md)). The path for Gemma is to save the adapter in PEFT form, convert it with llama.cpp's `convert_lora_to_gguf.py`, and load it with `llama-server --lora` ([llama.cpp](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/convert_lora_to_gguf.py)). The adapter would be trained against an MLX quantization of the model but applied to the QAT q4_0 GGUF, a mismatch whose effect is unmeasured; the check is the gold v2 answer eval with and without `--lora`, which is also the kill switch.

**Data it would need.** RAFT-style examples: a question, the gold passage mixed with 4 distractors (and about 20% of examples with no gold passage, which should decline), and an answer that quotes and cites. RAFT on Llama2-7B found training with distractors beat training on the gold passage alone, and that a gold-passage share around 80% worked well ([Zhang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.10131)). Gold v2 cannot be training data. Answers would come from Gemma itself on bank-style generated questions, filtered by the support judge, and from the slower pipelines (iterative retrieval, summary-first) distilled into single-pass behavior. Without an external teacher, the ceiling is roughly "Gemma on its best pipeline, consistently".

**What it could improve.** Behavior: fewer over-cautious declines when the gold passage is present (18 answerable gold items declined), the policy-line register, citation format, "stated vs inferred" marking. RAFT's headline gains (HotpotQA 35.28 against 4.41 for domain fine-tuning plus RAG) start from an untuned Llama2-7B that could barely follow a RAG prompt; Gemma 4 12B instruct already scores 0.958 to 0.971 faithfulness and 15/15 declines on gold v2, so the headroom is much smaller. Self-RAG's reflection tokens needed a purpose-trained 7B or 13B model and a critic ([Asai et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11511)); building that data locally is a project of its own.

**Not for facts.** Fine-tuning on the corpus to make Gemma "know" the lore is the wrong tool: RAG consistently beat unsupervised fine-tuning for both known and new knowledge ([Ovadia et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.05934)), and fine-tuning on new facts is learned slowly and linearly increases hallucination as it is learned ([Gekhman et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.05904)). This matches the roadmap's earlier rejection.

**Cost and verdict.** 1,000 examples of about 4,000 tokens for 2 epochs is 8 million tokens; at 150 to 250 tokens per second for a 12B model on the M5 Pro (extrapolated, unmeasured) that is 9 to 15 hours of training, plus about 6 hours to generate and filter the data, plus a gold run and a probe run per adapter. Against experiments 1.1 to 1.6, which together cost under 25 hours and target the same declines directly, the LoRA is not worth doing now. Revisit only if, after those, a measured behavior gap remains that three prompt variants fail to close.

## 3. What not to do

**Contextual retrieval on the embedded text.** Anthropic's 35% (embeddings) and 49% (embeddings plus BM25) failure-rate reductions were measured with Claude-written context and their chosen embedders ([Anthropic 2024](https://www.anthropic.com/news/contextual-retrieval)). On Trevor, a prefix on embedded text cost dense recall@10 0.087 (story prefix) and 0.044 (model-written line), and the model-written line added +0.000 over the free prefix on hybrid (baseline section 8.1). Measured and refuted twice; do not spend the 9.1 hours.

**Full GraphRAG.** Microsoft's GraphRAG was built for global sensemaking questions ("main themes of the dataset") over about 1 million tokens and measured on comprehensiveness and diversity ([Edge et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130)). In a standardized comparison, community GraphRAG gained 5 to 6 points on multi-hop with a 70B reader but lost to plain RAG on single-hop NQ with an 8B reader (63.01 against 64.78 F1), and KG-triplet GraphRAG reached only 34.28 F1 on NQ because 65.8% of answer entities made it into the graph ([Han et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.11371)). Trevor's relationship category is already its best (0.83), its identity links showed that extraction errors are amplified by graph union (a quote-only address merged Raidian and Serafina into Kal'tsit, baseline section 13), and entity extraction over 15,762 chunks at about 2.4 s each is 10 hours before community summaries. Trevor already has the useful parts of a graph (identity links, a 5,911-speaker index, dossiers, event summaries). If multi-hop stays weak after 1.8, HippoRAG-style Personalized PageRank over the speaker co-occurrence graph (free, no extraction) is the cheap test, not GraphRAG.

**HyDE or Query2doc as default query expansion.** Their gains are against unsupervised or lexical retrievers and rely on the LLM's world knowledge; Gemma's closed-book Arknights accuracy is 0.130 and gte is supervised (section 1.6). Use rewrites only as a retry on declines.

**Feeding much more context.** Beyond the 1.5 sweep, going to 32k or more tokens is where the inverted U and the lost-in-the-middle effects bite, measured on 9B to 12B models ([Jin et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.05983)), and it would slow every answer.

**Deep reranking with a small cross-encoder.** Measured negative on Trevor at depth 40 and consistent with [Jacob et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11767).

**Fine-tuning for knowledge, or a 50,000-question bank.** The first is refuted by the literature in section 2; the second was priced at 19 hours for coverage that nobody asks for (HANDOFF section 4). Grow the bank from the real question mix and miss logs (1.3).

**Scene summaries and more flat summary units.** Refuted on Trevor (section 9.1), and P3b profile units displaced story passages for 2 gold questions; add summaries by route (1.4), not to the flat index.

## 4. Evaluation practice for these experiments

**The probe measures behavior, not correctness.** "Responds" counts a wrong answer that addresses the question. Two cheap upgrades: (1) for the 68 story-answerable probe questions, collect reference answers from the top comments of the same Reddit threads through the same Arctic Shift archive the titles came from, and have the judge compare answer to reference (noisy, but the first correctness number on real questions); (2) Ian labels 40 to 60 probe verdicts (responds and one faithfulness sentence each), and the judge's agreement is reported as Cohen's kappa, not percent agreement, because judges with high percent agreement can still assign very different scores and show a leniency bias ([Thakur et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12624)). Only the best and largest judges approached human agreement there; GPT-4 reached over 80% agreement with humans in MT-Bench, the human-human level ([Zheng et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)), which says nothing about a 9B judge until it is measured. ARES showed a few hundred human labels are enough to correct a model judge's estimates with prediction-powered inference ([Saad-Falcon et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09476)).

**Reference-free metrics.** RAGAS-style faithfulness and context relevance ([Es et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15217)) are what Trevor already computes; keep them, but never let the same model filter and grade (1.9), and avoid Gemma judging its own answers (self-enhancement bias, Zheng et al.).

**Power.** On 120 paired questions, P3b's +0.025 was 4 gained and 1 lost with a CI spanning zero. To see a +0.05 effect reliably, grow the probe to about 300 (add the Discord questions, which were never used to build anything) and report McNemar on discordant pairs as the harness already does for gold v2. Keep the Discord set as the held-out check for anything tuned on Reddit questions (routes, bank clusters, policy keywords).

**Gate order per experiment.** Retrieval-only numbers first (seconds), then gold v2 answers (paired, unanswerable must stay 15/15), then the probe, then `scripts/route-check.py` (26/26 today). Every new flag's off value must reproduce the previous outputs exactly (house rule), checked by prompt hash or output diff.

## Measured on this Mac: should Trevor be fine-tuned? (2026-09-29)

Ian asked to measure the time and decide. A LoRA timing pilot with mlx-lm (0.31.3 and main at 0.31.4.dev131), rank 8, 16 layers, gradient checkpointing, answer-only loss, batch 1, on 124 training examples in `ask`'s exact format (system prompt, retrieved passages, question, and an answer judged correct and fully supported: gold set v2 answers and judged bank answers):

- **Gemma 4 12B cannot be trained with mlx-lm.** The MLX build (`mlx-community/gemma-4-12B-it-4bit`) is the multimodal `gemma4_unified` model type, which mlx-lm does not support in the release or in main; the pilot used Gemma 3 12B 4-bit, the same size class, as the timing proxy.
- **Trevor's real prompts do not fit.** At a maximum sequence of 10,240 tokens (`ask` prompts run 7,900 tokens median, 9,445 max) the run failed with Metal out-of-memory on the 24 GB machine.
- **Prompts cut to about 3,000 tokens fit**: 12 steps in 172 s including load and two validation passes, about 12 s per step, 8.2 GB peak resident memory; validation loss 2.905 to 0.877 over the 12 steps, so the setup trains. (At 4,096 with full prompts, truncation cut the answers off and the loss was over 0 tokens: a trap for any data built from long prompts.)

Cost of a real run at those numbers: 1,000 to 1,500 examples at about 30 s each to generate and judge (8 to 12 h), 2 epochs at about 12 s per step (7 to 10 h), then a GGUF LoRA conversion for llama-server; about 15 to 22 hours of Mac time. Against it: the model would train on 3,000-token prompts and serve 9,000-token ones (or `ask` would have to cut its context and lose passages it finds now: the gold chunk reaches the model on 104 of 131 gold questions at the current budget); it can change behavior, not knowledge, and fine-tuning on facts raises hallucination (section 2); and what is left to fix is mostly retrieval and missing sources, not behavior (faithfulness 0.946 to 0.971 on gold set v2, unanswerable questions declined 15 of 15). **Decision: do not fine-tune now.** Revisit only if a measured behavior failure remains after the retrieval work, with a model mlx-lm supports and prompts under about 3,000 tokens. The pilot's downloads (13 GB) and environment were deleted afterwards.
